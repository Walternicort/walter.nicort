My first photograph

The story of my first photograph is actually quite recent. I was helping one of my favorite people in the world with her master’s project. We were collecting samples from ducks, looking for zoonotic viruses in a lake a couple of hours from Mexico City, in a place called Lerma.

At that time, a friend had lent me a camera to learn with, a Nikon D3100. The camera’s light meter wasn’t working, so I was trying to take a photo, whether it was a good one or a bad one, I didn’t care. Back then, I knew nothing about ISO, exposure time, or aperture. As a result, all I got were images of light and darkness.

Defeated, I put the camera back in my backpack and continued with the scientific work.

At the end of the sampling, when we were ready to head back to Mexico City, I saw an old man in a canoe on the lake. In that instant, I saw everything, the picture, the frame, the composition, everything. I quickly pulled out the camera, adjusted the settings as best as I could, and took the shot.

To my surprise, the image I had seen in my mind was exactly what appeared on the camera screen. To this day, I do not know if that moment was luck, destiny, or something else. But I do know this: I seek the images I already see in my mind before I capture them.
